02 / Bingo! Mad Angles

Making a snack behave like fan culture.

A social strategy built around football rivalry, real-time reactions and UGC, designed to make the brand participate in culture instead of interrupting it.

ProjectFIFA content strategy + consumption occasions
My roleCampaign strategy, content architecture, pitch
CompanyJuiceLabs.ai / ITC

Culture → rivalry → participation → repeatable brand behaviour

TL;DR

Problem

Football culture is crowded and brand intrusion is easy to spot.

Idea

Give Mad Angles repeatable behaviours that already feel native to fandom.

My contribution

Culture research, campaign architecture, social formats and pitch development.

Result

Strategic systems were taken through client pitching and approval.

Context

Indian football audiences participate intensely even when India is not on the field. The real action is distributed across memes, fan pages, rivalries, creator reactions, group chats, comments and second-screen behaviour.

That meant the opportunity was not simply to “make football content.” It was to give Mad Angles a role inside the behaviour that already exists.

The problem

Most brands enter tournament culture by borrowing football visuals and posting around match moments. That creates topical content, but not necessarily cultural relevance.

The brand did not need more football posts. It needed a recognisable way to behave during football.

My role

I owned / drove

  • Cultural and platform research
  • Campaign territories and repeatable content pillars
  • UGC and participation mechanics
  • Pitch narrative and client presentation
  • Consumption-occasion strategy

What I did not claim

  • No invented reach or engagement metrics
  • No claim of owning all downstream production
  • No suggestion that the brand became an official football property

Constraints

The work had to feel native to the conversation without becoming generic sports advertising. It also needed enough structure to be repeatable across a long tournament, not just one match.

Strategy

The campaign was built as a set of behaviours rather than a single big idea.

01

The Triangle Take

A reactive format designed to give the brand a sharp, recognisable take immediately after major match moments.

02

Fan Wars Fuel

A rivalry engine where the brand plays the chaotic-neutral friend who keeps every fan camp arguing.

03

Infiltration

Content designed to travel through fan pages, creators, comments, DMs, Stories and UGC, rather than depending only on the brand handle.

Consumption

A second strategic track looked beyond football and asked how Mad Angles could enter more eating occasions.

The behavioural observation was simple: people already create unexpected food combinations. The opportunity was to make those combinations socially shareable.

Turn weird combinations from a private habit into something people publicly flex.
01

Ignite

Seed surprising combinations that make people react.

02

Participate

Invite users to submit their own “Yeh Kya Combo Hai?!” moments.

03

Amplify

Use creator reactions and recurring content to make participation visible.

04

Reward

Close the loop by recognising the most entertaining or unexpected combinations.

Exploration

Community

Rivalry-first

Formats that make comments and fan arguments part of the creative.

UGC

Behaviour-first

Turn existing eating habits into repeatable audience participation.

The trap we avoided

Making the brand “look like football” without giving it a reason to exist in football conversation. The strategy prioritised behaviour over borrowed aesthetics.

Outcome

A campaign system, not a one-post idea.

The strongest evidence available is that the strategic territories were pitched and approved. I am intentionally not attaching unverified reach or engagement numbers to the case.

Reflection

Keep: build brands around repeatable behaviours instead of isolated posts.

Change: with live performance access, I would establish a response framework by match type, rivalry intensity and format speed.

Test next: compare creator-led rivalry content against brand-handle reactive formats for comment depth and share rate.
