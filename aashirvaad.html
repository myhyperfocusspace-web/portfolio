03 / Aashirvaad Soya Chunks × Sunrise

Turning one TVC into a reusable promo system.

A 15-second Bengali TVC adaptation where the real challenge was not only the creative idea, but keeping production coherent while inputs, pack details, VO, colour and motion direction continued to evolve.

TL;DR

Problem

Add a free-product offer without making the existing TVC feel patched together.

Idea

Use the food world itself as the transition into a fixed promo system.

My contribution

Execution, asset regeneration, motion exploration, VO sync, revisions and delivery.

Result

A delivered 15-second film plus a reusable promotional template.

Context

Aashirvaad already had a Bengali Soya Chunks TVC. The requirement was to repurpose that film into a templatized 15-second promotional system that could introduce a free Sunrise Garam Masala ₹5 pack and remain reusable for future promotional swaps.

This was a production adaptation problem more than a blank-page campaign problem. The existing film, brand mnemonic and product story had to remain intact while the new offer became clear and ownable.

The problem

A hard cut from appetising food footage to a generic offer card would make the promo feel bolted on. At the same time, the offer needed enough screen time to communicate product quantity, partner pack and “free” mechanic clearly.

The promo needed to feel like the TVC naturally became an offer, not like an offer interrupted the TVC.

My role

My responsibility was primarily on the execution and project-management side. I was not claiming ownership of every upstream strategic decision or of the original master TVC.

I owned / drove

  • Storyboard-to-production execution
  • AI asset generation and regeneration
  • Product integration and pack corrections
  • Animation direction exploration
  • Bengali VO fit and timing
  • Revision management and final delivery

Project leadership

  • Translated changing client inputs into workable next steps
  • Managed production blockers from delayed approvals
  • Kept product and offer accuracy under review
  • Protected the deadline while scope kept moving

Constraints

This project accumulated real production constraints after initial alignment:

01

Storyboard changes after approval

Previously aligned assets and sequences had to be revisited and regenerated.

02

Product accuracy changes

The pack setup changed and a generated mini pack incorrectly showed 6g instead of 5g, requiring correction.

03

Motion direction was not locked

The initial style was considered old-school, leading to roughly six animation explorations.

04

VO and sound direction evolved

Dummy VO, Bengali approval, jingle retention and SFX hierarchy all changed during production.

05

Brand system inputs were incomplete

Background and colour direction were initially inferred from the TVC and later reworked after feedback.

Strategy bridge

The key visual bridge was to make the existing food world generate the promotional system.

Existing storyMasala-coated soya + gravy drip
→
Transition cueDrip becomes red-gold ribbon
→
Promo systemSunrise reveal + offer + Aashirvaad end frame

The ribbon then becomes a reusable piece of motion language, giving future promo swaps a fixed visual container.

Execution

The final storyboard moved through seven core beats inside the 15-second window:

01

Pack setup

Aashirvaad hero presence with early promo context.

02

Hydration proof

Dry chunks into water, followed by squeeze and drip.

03

Food beauty

Chunks enter curry, masala coats the product, spoon shot slows the pace.

04

Promo reveal

The gravy drip becomes the red-gold ribbon that introduces Sunrise Garam Masala ₹5.

05

Offer architecture

Aashirvaad pack, support pack, quantity mechanic and free offer are staged in sequence.

06

End slate

Ribbon/bow system resolves into the existing brand mnemonic.

The version that did not work

Motion that felt too old-school

The first animation direction did not match the expected contemporary feel. Instead of defending the execution, I treated the feedback as a production reset and explored around six alternative motion treatments before the style was locked.

This became a useful operating lesson: when motion language is subjective, get explicit style alignment before producing too far downstream.

Outcome

A complicated production process still ended in a usable system.

The final film and reusable promotional template were delivered on schedule. The case is less about claiming a single “big idea” and more about demonstrating adaptive project leadership under changing requirements.

Reflection

Keep: modular thinking. The fixed ribbon, colour, motion and end-frame system makes future product swaps easier.

Change: lock animation reference, pack configuration, brand palette and VO language earlier through a single pre-production checklist.

Test next: build a small pre-approved motion library so future promo variants can be generated without re-opening style exploration.
