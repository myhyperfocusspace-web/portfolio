01 / Sunfeast Dark Fantasy

Making taste visible before trial.

An amplification strategy built around a simple challenge: how do you reduce a bitterness perception without turning a premium cookie into defensive advertising?

TL;DR

Problem

“Dark” cues risked making the product feel bitter before trial.

Idea

Make the reaction to the cookie more persuasive than an explanation.

My contribution

Translate the barrier into amplification routes, hooks and creator thinking.

Result

The strategic route moved into final execution and live assets.

Context

Dark Fantasy already had a premium, rich and indulgent brand world. The amplification brief was not about rebuilding the brand. It was about helping recruit aware non-trialists, especially where penetration was lower, while preserving that sophistication.

The tension was specific: the name, historically dark packaging and dark chocolate crème could create an assumption that the cookie would taste like dark chocolate and therefore feel bitter.

The problem

A direct rebuttal would solve the wrong problem. Saying “it is not bitter” would drag the communication into the very association the brand wanted to move past.

The more useful question became: what can a potential trialist see that makes the taste feel undeniable before they personally try it?

People did not need a defence of the cookie. They needed visible evidence of pleasure.

My role

I worked across the strategy and execution bridge rather than only writing a deck or only producing assets.

I owned / drove

  • Strategic interpretation of the brief
  • Consumer barrier translation
  • Amplification routes and social hooks
  • Influencer / creator thinking
  • Client alignment and execution development

Important boundary

  • I am not claiming authorship of the master TVC
  • My work focused on amplification around the existing campaign thought
  • No campaign performance numbers are claimed without verified data

Constraints

The work had to recruit trialists without making the brand loud, slapstick or overly explanatory. Dark Fantasy still needed to feel premium, international, young and sophisticated.

Strategy

The strategic territory became what the deck framed as Expression Contagion: visible reactions make taste socially legible.

Instead of opening with product claims, the creative could open with human behaviour, curiosity and involuntary expressions. The Choco Fills reveal then becomes the payoff.

01

Hook with behaviour

Start with a reaction or pattern viewers want explained.

02

Build curiosity

Let the audience wonder what causes that reaction instead of immediately making a taste claim.

03

Resolve through the product

Use the Choco Fills reveal as the reason behind the expression.

Exploration

Different social routes could carry the same strategic principle without repeating one execution.

Route A

Reaction-led reels

Human expressions first, product explanation second.

Route B

Creator hooks

Curiosity formats built around what the cookie visibly does to people.

Route C

TVC repurposing

Extract and reshape campaign moments for social-first attention patterns.

The route I deliberately avoided

Over-explaining that the product “is not bitter.” Even if factually useful, it would force the brand to repeat the negative frame instead of replacing it with appetite and desire.

Outcome

Strategy that could survive the jump from deck to social execution.

The route progressed into final creative assets. The most credible result I can claim from the material available is approval and execution, not unverified reach, conversion or sales metrics.

Reflection

Keep: start with the actual consumer tension, then protect the brand tone while solving it.

Change: if performance data were available, I would compare which hook type created the strongest trial intent or completion rate.

Test next: reaction-led creator formats versus product-first appetite formats across lower-penetration markets.
